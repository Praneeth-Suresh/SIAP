Belief, Uncertainty, and Decision

The competent agent does not worship certainty; it manages uncertainty with discipline. Belief is a computational object, not a mood.

Bayesian formalization

With latent state (s_t), history (h_t), and belief (b_t(s)=P(s_t=s\mid h_t)), updates follow [ b_{t+1}(s') = \eta\,\Omega(o_{t+1}\mid s')\sum_{s}T(s'\mid s,a_t)b_t(s), ] where (\eta) normalizes. Decision quality is evaluated through belief-state value: [ V^\pi(b)=\mathbb{E}!\left[\sum_{t\ge 0}\gamma^t R(s_t,a_t)\mid b_0=b,\pi\right]. ]

Information gathering is itself an action. The value of information for query (q) is [ \operatorname{VoI}(q)=\mathbb{E}!\left[\max_a \mathbb{E}[U(a,s)\mid h_t,q]\right] \;-\;\max_a \mathbb{E}[U(a,s)\mid h_t]. ]

Three-theory braid

  1. Bayesian decision theory. Rational action under uncertainty is posterior-weighted expected utility [34] [7].
  2. Information theory. Entropy and mutual information quantify epistemic uncertainty and expected reduction from sensing/retrieval actions [35].
  3. Proper scoring and calibration. Probabilistic outputs must be evaluated with strictly proper rules to avoid overconfident nonsense dressed as confidence [49].

How to study this formally

Run ablations where retrieval, tool probes, or clarification questions are treated as costly information actions. Measure calibration (ECE/Brier), downstream utility, and VoI realized per unit latency/token cost. If epistemic actions do not improve calibrated decision quality, the loop is decorative, not deliberative.
