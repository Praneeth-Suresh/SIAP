Equilibria, Self-Play, and Learning Dynamics

Equilibrium is the grammar of strategic stability: who can deviate, who gains, and when dynamics settle or cycle.

Formal game model

In a normal-form game ((N,{A_i},{u_i})), mixed strategy profile (\sigma^{}) is Nash if [ u_i(\sigma_i^{},\sigma_{-i}^{}) \ge u_i(\sigma_i,\sigma_{-i}^{})\quad \forall i,\forall \sigma_i. ] For sequential interaction, Markov games extend MDPs with multi-agent action tuples and payoff vectors.

Three-theory braid

  1. Nash equilibrium analysis. Fixed points characterize unilateral stability and expose incentive misalignment [19].
  2. Self-play in Markov games. Repeated interaction drives policy co-adaptation and emergent conventions [20] [21].
  3. Learning dynamics. Fictitious play and no-regret dynamics explain convergence to correlated/coarse-correlated equilibria in repeated games [65] [64] [63].

How to study this formally

Track exploitability, diversity collapse, and equilibrium selection under perturbation. Evaluate whether local agent improvements increase system-level welfare or merely shift failure to other roles. If strategic dynamics are ignored, “multi-agent” is just parallel monologue.
