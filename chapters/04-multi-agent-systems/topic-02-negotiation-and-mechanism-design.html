Negotiation and Mechanism Design

Negotiation is what happens when autonomy meets scarcity. Mechanism design is how we stop that meeting from turning into chaos.

Formal mechanism frame

A mechanism is [ \mathcal{M}=(N,\Theta, A, g, p), ] with agents (N), types (\Theta), outcome space (A), allocation rule (g), and transfers (p).
Desired properties: incentive compatibility (IC), individual rationality (IR), and budget feasibility.

For truthful dominant-strategy reporting, each agent (i) should satisfy [ u_i!\big(g(\hat\theta_i,\hat\theta_{-i}),p_i(\hat\theta_i,\hat\theta_{-i});\theta_i\big) \ge u_i!\big(g(\theta'i,\hat\theta),p_i(\theta'i,\hat\theta);\theta_i\big). ]

Three-theory braid

  1. VCG-style efficient mechanisms. Transfer rules can align local truthfulness with global welfare [57] [58] [59].
  2. Social-choice limits. Gibbard-Satterthwaite impossibility reminds us that manipulation-free voting is highly constrained [60] [61].
  3. Contract-net negotiation. Task delegation via bidding provides practical distributed allocation under heterogeneous capabilities [62].

How to study this formally

Measure allocative efficiency, truthfulness regret, and fairness under strategic reporting. Simulate adversarial type misreports and collusion. A negotiation protocol without incentive analysis is merely an argument with extra JSON.
