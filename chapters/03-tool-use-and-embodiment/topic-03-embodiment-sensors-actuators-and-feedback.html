Embodiment: Sensors, Actuators, and Feedback

Embodiment means the world can answer back. Even software-only agents are embodied in systems with latency, saturation, and irreversible side effects.

State-space formalization

Let continuous latent state (x_t), control (u_t), and observation (o_t): [ x_{t+1}=f(x_t,u_t)+w_t,\qquad o_t=g(x_t)+v_t. ] The controller chooses (u_t=\pi(o_{0:t},u_{0:t-1})) to optimize long-run utility under actuator and safety bounds.

Sensor quality enters through observability; actuator quality enters through controllability and delay.

Three-theory braid

  1. Control-theoretic state estimation. Kalman-style filtering formalizes noisy sensing and recursive belief updates [36].
  2. Reinforcement learning dynamics. MDP/POMDP and return optimization model delayed consequences of action [3] [4].
  3. System-level environment coupling. Agent loops embedded in real infrastructures require explicit modeling of latency, rate limits, and non-stationarity [2].

How to study this formally

Quantify sensor noise sensitivity, actuator saturation failures, and closed-loop stability under delays. Evaluate with perturbation tests and feedback-loop stress scenarios. An embodied agent that ignores feedback geometry is not autonomous; it is merely loud.
