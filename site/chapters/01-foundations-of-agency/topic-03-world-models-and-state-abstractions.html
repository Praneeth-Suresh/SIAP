World Models and State Abstractions

To model a world is to choose what to forget without forgetting what matters. Abstraction is compression under decision pressure.

Formalization

Let (\phi:\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{Z}) map ground states to abstract states. A homomorphic abstraction preserves control-relevant structure: [ R(s,a)=\bar{R}(\phi(s),a),\qquad \sum_{s':\phi(s')=z'} T(s'\mid s,a)=\bar{T}(z'\mid \phi(s),a). ] Then planning in (\mathcal{Z}) approximates planning in (\mathcal{S}), with error bounded by abstraction distortion.

For causal world models, variables (X) follow structural equations (X_i=f_i(\mathrm{Pa}(X_i),U_i)). Interventions (\mathrm{do}(X_j=x)) define action semantics beyond passive prediction [6].

Three-theory braid

  1. State abstraction in MDPs. Homomorphisms characterize when compressed states retain optimal-control content [37].
  2. Bisimulation metrics. Behavioral similarity can be quantified continuously, giving principled abstraction error control [38].
  3. Causal modeling. Structural causal models distinguish observation from intervention, crucial for tool-using agents that change their environment [6].

How to study this formally

Estimate value-loss (\Delta V = |V^{} - \tilde{V}^{}\circ\phi|), intervention accuracy under held-out counterfactuals, and policy robustness to distribution shift. In short: if abstraction gains speed but loses action relevance, you have invented a beautifully efficient hallucination engine.
