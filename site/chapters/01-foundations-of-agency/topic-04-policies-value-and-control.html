Policies, Value, and Control

A policy is not merely “what the model says next”; it is the constitution that governs action under uncertainty, delay, and cost.

Core equations

For policy (\pi), [ V^\pi(s)=\mathbb{E}\pi!\left[\sum\gamma^t r_t\mid s_0=s\right],\qquad Q^\pi(s,a)=\mathbb{E}\pi!\left[\sum\gamma^t r_t\mid s_0=s,a_0=a\right]. ] Optimality obeys Bellman recursion: [ V^{}(s)=\max_a\left{R(s,a)+\gamma\sum_{s'}T(s'\mid s,a)V^{}(s')\right}. ]

When policies are parameterized by (\theta), gradient ascent uses [ \nabla_\theta J(\theta)=\mathbb{E}{\pi\theta}!\left[\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a\mid s)\,Q^{\pi_\theta}(s,a)\right]. ]

Three-theory braid

  1. Dynamic programming. Bellman equations provide exact structure for value and control decomposition [5] [39].
  2. Policy-gradient methods. Stochastic policies can be optimized directly with unbiased gradient estimators [40].
  3. Model predictive control (MPC). Receding-horizon optimization handles constraints and changing dynamics in real deployments [41].

How to study this formally

Treat orchestration as layered control: proposal policy, adjudication policy, and oversight policy. Compare designs on return, stability, and safety-constraint adherence under perturbations. A policy that looks brilliant on one trajectory but violates constraints under mild shift is theater, not control.
