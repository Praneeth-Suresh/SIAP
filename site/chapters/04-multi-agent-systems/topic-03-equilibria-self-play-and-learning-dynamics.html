Equilibria, Self-Play, and Learning Dynamics

Equilibrium is the grammar of strategic stability: who can deviate, who gains, and when dynamics settle or cycle.

Formal game model

In a normal-form game ((N,{A_i},{u_i})), mixed strategy profile (\sigma^{}) is Nash if [ u_i(\sigma_i^{},\sigma_{-i}^{}) \ge u_i(\sigma_i,\sigma_{-i}^{})\quad \forall i,\forall \sigma_i. ] For sequential interaction, Markov games extend MDPs with multi-agent action tuples and payoff vectors.

A useful robustness metric is exploitability: [ \mathcal{E}(\sigma)=\sum_i\left(\max_{\sigma_i'}u_i(\sigma_i',\sigma_{-i})-u_i(\sigma)\right), ] which is zero only at exact equilibrium in two-player zero-sum settings.

Repeated adaptation is governed by regret: [ R_T^i=\max_a\sum_{t=1}^T u_i(a,a_{-i}^t)-\sum_{t=1}^T u_i(a_i^t,a_{-i}^t), ] and no-regret learning drives empirical play toward correlated equilibrium sets.

Sequential strategic control

For Markov games, value recursion for player (i) under profile (\pi) is [ V_i^\pi(s)=\mathbb{E}{a\sim \pi}!\left[r_i(s,a)+\gamma\sumP(s'|s,a)V_i^\pi(s')\right], ] with equilibrium planning replacing unilateral maximization by strategic best-response consistency [20] [5].

Three-theory braid

Nash equilibrium analysis gives fixed-point notions of unilateral stability, self-play in Markov games gives the adaptive process by which policies co-evolve under repeated interaction, and learning-dynamics tools like fictitious play and no-regret updates characterize when trajectories converge, cycle, or fragment, so multi-agent intelligence appears as a braid of foresight (optimize expected utility against strategic responses), responsiveness (update policies from observed opponent behavior), and resilience (maintain low exploitability and bounded regret under perturbations) [19] [20] [21] [63] [64] [65] [33] [34].

How to study this formally

Track exploitability, regret curves, diversity collapse, and equilibrium selection stability under perturbation. If strategic dynamics are ignored, "multi-agent" is just parallel monologue.
